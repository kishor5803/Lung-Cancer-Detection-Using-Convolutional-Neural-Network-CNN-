Histopathological Subtyping vs. Clinical Cancer Staging

LUNG-AI's CNN Model evaluates microscopic cellular architecture (H&E stain) to classify the tissue subtype: Benign Lung Tissue, Lung Adenocarcinoma, or Lung Squamous Cell Carcinoma. Once malignancy is confirmed microscopically, oncologists utilize anatomical radiographic imaging (CT, PET-CT, MRI) and lymph node biopsies to determine the Clinical Cancer Stage (0–IV) to guide surgical resectability, chemoradiation, and systemic targeted therapy.

Connecting AI Histopathology with Clinical Staging

How deep learning classification directly connects with patient diagnostic pathways in healthcare systems.

Step 1

Microscopic Tissue Biopsy

A core needle biopsy or bronchoalveolar lavage sample is obtained and stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E).

Step 2 (LUNG-AI)

CNN Subtype Classification

Our deep learning models (Custom CNN, MobileNetV2, EfficientNetB0) evaluate cellular morphology and output subtype confidence (Adenocarcinoma vs Squamous vs Benign) with Grad-CAM heatmaps.

Step 3

Radiographic Staging (TNM)

Once cancer is identified under microscopy, whole-body 18F-FDG PET-CT and brain MRI establish the physical stage (0 to IV).

Step 4

Personalized Tumor Board Plan

Multidisciplinary oncology teams combine the histological subtype with the clinical stage to prescribe surgery, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy.
